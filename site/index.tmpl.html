How the letters were chosen

Five steps, every number from the scripts in the repository.

  1. 1Measure what I type

    Every message I ever typed to an AI: the Claude Code history and the claude.ai export. Pasted text, code, URLs and logs thrown out, duplicates removed, each message tagged by its stop words. 1,677,299 characters remained.

    I weighted French up to a third of the mix, because my work writing is French and none of it was in the sample.

    The eight letters I type most, and their share of every keystroke.

  2. 2Price every key

    The geometry is beekeeb’s own firmware file: the column stagger, the finger that owns each column, each finger’s home key. A key costs one plus its distance from home, times 1.6 under a pinky, 1.2 under a ring finger, 1.3 on the inner index columns.

    Home keys cost 1. The far corners cost 3.2.

    The cost of each of the thirty core keys. Darker is dearer.

  3. 3Define the score

    Lower is better: each term is a share of my letter pairs or triples, times a weight I picked. Other weights give another board; a first run without finger caps put 16% on each ring finger. That caveat is the method.

    Points = share × weight × 100: 1.39% on one finger × 3 = 4.18. They add up to the score.

  4. 4Search by annealing

    Start from a random board. Swap two keys, score again. Better: keep it. Worse: keep it with a probability that falls as the run cools.

    Below, seed 5’s run, 132 down to 25.5. Early, one swap in six is kept although it hurts; late, none. Eight runs scored 480,000 of the 30! boards, 2.65 × 1032.

  5. 5Pick, and say why

    Three seeds tied within five thousandths. Seed 1 mirrors seed 5, so the vowels went right, the convention.
